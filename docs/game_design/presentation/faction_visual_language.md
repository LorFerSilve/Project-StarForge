# Faction Visual Language

> **Status:** Draft  
> **Authority:** Visual identity, architecture, equipment language, signage, silhouettes, material emphasis, and presentation boundaries for major factions and the Continuance

## 1. Purpose

Major factions must be identifiable through coherent physical design rather than through UI color coding alone.

A player should often be able to infer who built, controls, repaired, or occupies a location from its visual language.

## 2. Shared Human Baseline

Human factions descend from related pre-Fracture technological standards.

They can therefore share:

- compatible docking conventions;
- human-scale access;
- industrial components;
- standardized interfaces where lore supports them.

Faction identity comes from how that common technological inheritance evolved.

## 3. Identification Channels

Faction identity uses multiple simultaneous channels:

- silhouette;
- architecture;
- materials;
- equipment proportions;
- insignia;
- signage/typography;
- lighting/accent color;
- maintenance condition;
- uniforms/robots/vehicles;
- spatial organization.

Color alone is never sufficient.

## 4. Helios Directorate

### Design Character

Disciplined, defensive, standardized, institutional.

### Forms

- angular armored geometry;
- repeated standardized modules;
- reinforced doors/checkpoints;
- clear hierarchy in space planning;
- compact protected infrastructure.

### Materials

- durable coated alloys;
- dark armor/composite surfaces;
- restrained clean service panels;
- controlled warning markings.

### Visual Impression

Order, authority, security, reliability, controlled force.

### Equipment

- robust ballistic/defensive systems;
- visible armor;
- formal access hardware;
- standardized weapon mounts.

### Signage

Regulatory, numbered, procedural, unambiguous.

## 5. Meridian Compact

### Design Character

Commercial, modular, logistics-heavy, practical, adaptable.

### Forms

- cargo-compatible modular architecture;
- wide loading corridors;
- external service frames;
- expandable market/warehouse structures;
- visible freight handling.

### Materials

- mixed industrial finishes;
- replaceable panels;
- commercial branding surfaces;
- more visible wear than Helios.

### Visual Impression

Movement, trade, access, interchangeability, economic activity.

### Equipment

- cargo manipulators;
- utility craft;
- flexible storage;
- multi-purpose industrial equipment.

### Signage

Route-oriented, inventory/market codes, vendor branding, multilingual commercial marks where authored.

## 6. Asterion Institute

### Design Character

Scientific, precise, instrument-focused, controlled experimentation.

### Forms

- cleaner geometry;
- enclosed instrument bays;
- observation surfaces;
- sensor arrays;
- deliberate symmetry/measurement references.

### Materials

- light composite/ceramic surfaces;
- clean alloys;
- glass/transparent containment;
- dense but organized instrumentation.

### Visual Impression

Analysis, precision, research, high information density.

### Equipment

- sensor arrays;
- laboratory apparatus;
- shielded sample handling;
- advanced control displays.

### Signage

Scientific notation, experiment IDs, hazard classifications, clear laboratory zoning.

## 7. Freehold Coalition

### Design Character

Independent, community-built, repaired, heterogeneous but functional.

### Forms

- repurposed pre-Fracture structures;
- visible reinforcement;
- mixed module origins;
- improvised but competent repairs;
- more open communal spaces where safe.

### Materials

- reused alloy;
- patched surfaces;
- locally manufactured components;
- varied paint/wear.

### Visual Impression

Survival, adaptation, community ownership, practical resilience.

### Equipment

- repaired legacy systems;
- mixed civilian/industrial technology;
- locally modified defense and utility hardware.

### Signage

Human, locally authored, sometimes hand-modified; still readable and functional.

## 8. Blackwake Corsairs

### Design Character

Aggressive salvage culture, mobile, opportunistic, intimidating, mechanically improvised.

### Forms

- asymmetrical additions;
- external armor patches;
- exposed weapon mounts;
- boarding infrastructure;
- repurposed cargo/ship components.

### Materials

- dark worn metal;
- scorched/repaired armor;
- salvaged parts from multiple origins;
- rougher exposed structure.

### Visual Impression

Predatory mobility, salvage, coercion, tactical improvisation.

### Equipment

- breaching hardware;
- jury-rigged weapons;
- boarding craft;
- visible ammunition/cargo handling.

### Signage

Crew marks, warnings, graffiti/insignia, practical tactical labels rather than standardized institutional systems.

## 9. Continuance

### Design Character

Distributed machine infrastructure, preserved legacy logic, non-human operational priorities.

### Forms

- precise machine-service geometry;
- repeated functional patterns;
- low emphasis on human comfort;
- embedded mechanisms;
- infrastructure integrated into architecture.

### Materials

- old but unusually preserved technical surfaces;
- exposed machine interfaces;
- restrained emissive state indicators;
- minimal decorative treatment.

### Visual Impression

Persistence, automation, alienated familiarity, infrastructure acting without human presence.

### Units

Continuance bodies should share control/sensor motifs but remain visually distinct from player-manufactured robot classes.

### Signage

Machine-readable markings, legacy maintenance symbols, sparse human labels where inherited.

## 10. Ancient Technology

Ancient technology is not a normal human faction.

It uses a separate visual language defined by Environment/Visual Direction:

- unfamiliar precise geometry;
- material continuity;
- sparse active surfaces;
- anomaly-linked behavior.

It must not simply look like a recolored Continuance facility.

## 11. Horizon Station

Horizon is player-controlled and historically pre-Fracture.

Its current identity becomes a layered combination of:

- original station fabric;
- player-manufactured systems;
- selectively integrated faction technology;
- repairs/customization.

It must never visually transform wholesale into the faction with which the player has highest reputation.

## 12. Faction Technology Integration

A faction-specific module installed on Horizon/ship/robot retains enough design language to communicate origin while adapting to the host platform's connection standards.

## 13. Uniforms and Characters

Character clothing/equipment should reflect:

- faction role;
- environment;
- profession;
- rank where relevant;
- material availability.

Faction membership should not be represented by identical uniform recolors across all jobs.

## 14. Security Readability

Guards/security units need clear role identification through:

- armor/equipment;
- posture/placement;
- insignia;
- weapon visibility;
- HUD identification where known.

## 15. Neutral/Hostile State

Faction visual identity is independent from current relationship state.

A Helios soldier does not turn red because Helios becomes hostile.

Hostility is communicated by:

- behavior;
- weapon posture;
- alarms;
- HUD marker/state;
- identified relationship information.

## 16. Faction-Controlled Architecture

A captured/occupied location can preserve original architecture while adding occupier layers:

- banners/insignia;
- checkpoints;
- equipment;
- lighting;
- security;
- repairs;
- cargo/use patterns.

Ownership change need not magically rebuild the structure.

## 17. Damage and Scarcity

Faction material condition reflects local world state.

A wealthy Meridian hub can be maintained while an isolated Meridian outpost is worn/understocked.

Faction style does not force every location to identical cleanliness.

## 18. Reputation Presentation Boundary

Reputation affects access, dialogue, services, hostility, etc., but does not globally recolor faction art.

UI can communicate current relation tier explicitly.

## 19. Color Accessibility

Each faction's accent palette is secondary to shape/material/signage identity.

Color-vision settings must not erase faction distinguishability.

## 20. Faction Iconography

Every major faction has a distinct insignia silhouette suitable for:

- signage;
- HUD markers;
- galaxy map;
- contracts;
- equipment origin.

Icons must remain recognizable in monochrome.

## 21. Technology Readability

Faction-specialized technology should visually communicate its engineering doctrine.

Examples:

- Helios: armor/defensive reliability;
- Meridian: logistics/modularity;
- Asterion: sensors/research/energy control;
- Freehold: repair/adaptation;
- Blackwake: breach/aggressive salvage;
- Continuance: autonomous infrastructure/control.

## 22. Mixed Faction Scenes

When multiple factions are present, silhouettes/signage must remain readable without requiring permanent floating nameplates.

HUD identification can appear on focus/scan/combat relevance.

## 23. Unknown Identity

If faction identity is genuinely unknown to the player, UI displays Unknown/Unidentified rather than leaking faction through metadata.

The art may still contain discoverable clues.

## 24. Procedural Content

Procedural locations/units must inherit the controlling faction's visual kit rules.

Random generation cannot mix incompatible faction architecture purely for variety unless the location fiction explicitly supports salvage/occupation history.

## 25. Explicit Non-Goals

No color-only factions, global reputation recolors, identical uniform models with palette swaps as the complete identity, or wholesale Horizon reskin based on alliance.

## 26. Tuneable Parameters

Exact palettes, insignia details, material roughness, wear, prop density, signage style, and accent-light intensity are art-direction tuneables within the fixed faction identities.

## 27. Dependencies

References GDS-7 Factions/Reputation, GDS-11 Raids, GDS-12 Economy/Reputation, Visual Direction, Environment Art, UI/HUD, and Accessibility.

## 28. Open Questions

None in the faction visual-language baseline.
