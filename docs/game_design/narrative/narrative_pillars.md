# Narrative Pillars

> **Status:** Design Complete  
> **Authority:** Core storytelling principles, narrative tone, player agency rules, mystery structure, and narrative/gameplay integration

## 1. Purpose

Narrative must reinforce the systems-driven identity of Project StarForge rather than sit beside gameplay as disconnected exposition.

## 2. Pillar One — Reconstruction Has Visible Consequences

The player's actions should make damaged systems, routes, communities, and the home station visibly more functional.

Narrative progress is expressed through:

- restored infrastructure;
- new survivors;
- safer routes;
- faction access;
- recovered knowledge;
- changed locations.

## 3. Pillar Two — Knowledge Is Progression

Discovering what happened during The Fracture must materially unlock:

- research;
- routes;
- blueprints;
- faction leverage;
- new objectives.

Lore should often be mechanically useful.

## 4. Pillar Three — Competence Over Destiny

The player is not important because of a prophecy, bloodline, or supernatural chosen-one status.

The player becomes important because they:

- recover the station;
- make difficult expeditions;
- build infrastructure;
- form relationships;
- solve technical problems;
- survive dangerous encounters.

## 5. Pillar Four — Competing Truths

Major factions interpret history through their own interests.

The player should encounter:

- incomplete records;
- biased testimony;
- conflicting recommendations.

The canon remains internally consistent even when characters disagree.

## 6. Pillar Five — No Simplistic Final Villain

The Fracture is the result of interacting technical, institutional, and anomalous failures.

The narrative can contain antagonists, but the core mystery does not resolve into one secret mastermind.

## 7. Pillar Six — The Player Remains Independent

The player can cooperate closely with factions without being forced to permanently join one.

The home station remains player-controlled.

## 8. Pillar Seven — Systems Create Story

Events such as:

- oxygen failure;
- stranded ship;
- damaged route relay;
- rescued survivor;
- faction blockade

should generate narrative context through the same systems used by normal gameplay.

## 9. Pillar Eight — Earned Optimism

The world is dangerous and damaged, but recovery is possible.

Successful play should visibly create:

- safer communities;
- functioning infrastructure;
- restored knowledge;
- increased cooperation.

## 10. Player Choice

Narrative choices can affect:

- reputation;
- local control;
- access;
- character relationships;
- faction support;
- endgame coalition.

They do not arbitrarily invalidate hours of unrelated progression.

## 11. No Fake Choice Requirement

A dialogue choice should not imply major consequence if both options resolve identically.

Cosmetic/roleplay dialogue choices may exist but must be presented as such through context rather than false stakes.

## 12. Branching Scope

The baseline uses **convergent branching**.

Major routes can differ in:

- ally;
- mission approach;
- faction support;
- optional characters;
- local outcomes.

The core Fracture mystery still reaches a shared late-game crisis so production scope remains manageable.

## 13. Failure

Routine mission failure can postpone or alter narrative progress.

Main-story critical content must provide a recovery/retry path.

The campaign is not permanently bricked by ordinary defeat.

## 14. Narrative Death

Permanent death of a recruited crew member is only allowed in an explicitly authored case that follows Global Rules.

The baseline main story does not require randomly killing recruited crew.

## 15. Exposition

Important information should be distributed across:

- dialogue;
- environment;
- data;
- mission events;
- system state.

Long exposition dumps are not the default.

## 16. Mystery Reveal

The Fracture mystery is revealed in layers:

1. consequences;
2. conflicting explanations;
3. technical evidence;
4. institutional records;
5. Continuance records;
6. Fracture Verge evidence;
7. final synthesis.

## 17. Player Knowledge vs World Knowledge

The player can know facts that not every faction knows.

This can create:

- dialogue options;
- leverage;
- warnings;
- alternate mission paths.

## 18. Narrative Rewards

Narrative progress can grant:

- route information;
- Research Evidence;
- Blueprints;
- reputation;
- crew/survivor opportunities;
- unique services;
- world-state changes.

It should not invent parallel progression currencies without another domain defining them.

## 19. Presentation

Narrative must remain understandable when the player returns after a long break.

Journal/objective summaries should record:

- what was learned;
- what remains unresolved;
- which factions are involved.

## 20. Explicit Non-Goals

Narrative does not require:

- chosen-one mythology;
- one binary morality meter;
- mandatory romance;
- massive cinematic dependency;
- fully branching separate campaigns;
- lore with no gameplay connection.

## 21. Dependencies

This specification depends on World Lore, Factions, Player Role, Main Story, Missions, Reputation, and Presentation.

## 22. Open Questions

None in the narrative-pillar baseline.
