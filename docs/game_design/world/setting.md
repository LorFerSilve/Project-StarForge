# World Setting

> **Status:** Draft  
> **Authority:** Canonical era, historical premise, civilization state, tone, inhabited-space baseline, and setting-wide facts

## 1. Core Premise

Project StarForge takes place in a fragmented interstellar civilization recovering from a catastrophic collapse known as **The Fracture**.

Before The Fracture, human civilization operated a dense network of colonies, stations, industrial systems, automated infrastructure, and long-range navigation corridors.

The Fracture broke that connected civilization into isolated regions.

## 2. Present Era

The game begins **67 years after The Fracture**.

This period is called **67 AF — After Fracture**.

The exact pre-Fracture calendar may appear in historical records, but AF is the primary gameplay-era notation.

## 3. Human Civilization

Humanity survives.

There was no total extinction event.

Instead, the former interstellar order fragmented into:

- isolated colonies;
- damaged orbital settlements;
- faction-controlled regions;
- abandoned stations;
- derelict fleets;
- automated facilities still operating without oversight;
- pirate-held routes;
- disconnected research sites;
- unexplored or unreachable former territories.

## 4. The Fracture

The Fracture involved simultaneous failures across the interstellar navigation and high-energy infrastructure network.

Observed consequences included:

- route collapse;
- communications loss;
- catastrophic navigation failures;
- station/ship losses;
- autonomous-system failures;
- regional isolation;
- political collapse;
- resource-chain disruption.

The public historical record does **not** contain one universally accepted cause.

## 5. Known Historical Facts

The following are established facts:

1. The failure was not a single ordinary military battle.
2. Multiple regions were affected within a short period.
3. Advanced route/navigation infrastructure was involved.
4. Pre-Fracture automated systems behaved abnormally in some locations.
5. Some anomalous phenomena appeared during or after the event.
6. Several major institutions destroyed, sealed, or hid records afterward.
7. Surviving factions preserve conflicting interpretations.

## 6. Unknown at Game Start

The player does not initially know:

- whether The Fracture was accidental;
- whether it was deliberately triggered;
- whether rogue machine intelligence was a cause, symptom, or responder;
- whether exotic/ancient technology contributed;
- whether all affected regions experienced the same event;
- whether the original threat remains active.

These are narrative mysteries, not implementation ambiguities.

## 7. Technological Level

The setting supports:

- orbital habitats;
- modular stations;
- advanced automation;
- practical robotics;
- fusion technology;
- high-energy shields;
- long-range space travel;
- advanced sensors;
- limited artificial intelligence;
- exotic late-game quantum/anomaly technology.

Technology is advanced but still resource- and infrastructure-dependent.

## 8. Technology Loss

Post-Fracture society does not universally understand or manufacture all pre-Fracture systems.

Some technology survives only as:

- recovered hardware;
- partial blueprints;
- restricted faction knowledge;
- damaged archives;
- research evidence;
- ancient/pre-Fracture artifacts.

This supports GDS-4 Research/Blueprint progression.

## 9. Inhabited Space

The playable galaxy represents a **bounded recovered region of human-inhabited space**, not the entire Milky Way.

The player gradually reconnects and explores strategically important sectors.

## 10. Scale Philosophy

The world should feel interstellar through:

- many strategic locations;
- varied systems;
- faction territories;
- historical depth;
- inaccessible routes that later open;
- long-range expeditions.

It does not require millions of simulated stars.

## 11. Home Station Context

The player's home station is a damaged pre-Fracture orbital facility that survived in dormant/degraded condition.

It occupies a strategically useful but initially peripheral location.

Its recovery gives the player:

- shelter;
- industrial foundation;
- research capability;
- a neutral operational base;
- access to old station records.

The exact station discovery is owned by Narrative.

## 12. Social Order

There is no unified galactic government.

Power is distributed among:

- regional factions;
- independent settlements;
- industrial operators;
- research institutions;
- pirate groups;
- autonomous machine systems.

## 13. Economy Context

Trade exists where routes and relations permit it.

Regional isolation means:

- some resources are locally abundant;
- others are strategic imports;
- advanced components can be scarce;
- recovered pre-Fracture technology can be highly valuable.

GDS-12 owns prices/economy.

## 14. Environmental Diversity

Playable locations can include:

- airless moons;
- frozen worlds;
- desert worlds;
- toxic planets;
- volcanic environments;
- overgrown abandoned colonies;
- asteroid belts;
- wreck fields;
- artificial habitats;
- alien/anomalous structures.

## 15. Non-Human Life

Non-human biological life exists.

It can range from simple ecosystems to dangerous native organisms.

The baseline does not require a large contemporary spacefaring alien empire.

## 16. Ancient/Unknown Technology

Some sites contain technology not attributable to known modern human factions.

At game start its origin is uncertain.

This technology is tied to:

- Anomaly Physics;
- Ancient Technology Cores;
- late-game navigation;
- main-story investigation.

## 17. Tone

The intended tone combines:

- frontier reconstruction;
- engineering competence;
- mystery;
- danger;
- discovery;
- earned optimism.

The setting is damaged, but not nihilistic.

The player's long-term actions visibly improve the world.

## 18. Moral Tone

Factions have conflicting interests and flaws.

The design avoids a baseline in which every institution is secretly identical or irredeemably evil.

Some conflicts have no perfect outcome.

## 19. Player Impact

The player can materially change regional conditions by:

- restoring routes;
- rescuing survivors;
- supporting settlements;
- defeating hostile infrastructure;
- recovering knowledge;
- forming faction relationships;
- building a powerful neutral station.

## 20. Explicit Non-Goals

The setting does not require:

- total human extinction;
- one unified empire;
- a seamless full Milky Way;
- a mandatory active alien empire;
- grimdark inevitability;
- post-apocalyptic technology behaving like magic without resource cost.

## 21. Dependencies

This specification is refined by Lore, Galaxy Structure, Factions, Narrative, Missions, Research, and Progression.

## 22. Open Questions

None in the setting baseline.
