# Station Progression

> **Status:** Design Complete  
> **Authority:** Horizon Station capability stages and the functional progression from damaged foothold to strategic nexus

## 1. Purpose

Station progression must represent real growth in infrastructure, redundancy, production, automation, defense, crew support, and strategic reach—not a hidden Base Level.

## 2. No Base Level

Horizon Station has no universal level that unlocks modules by itself.

Capability is derived from actual:

- constructed modules;
- connected infrastructure;
- Research/Blueprints;
- staffing/automation;
- operational resources;
- power/thermal/logistics capacity.

## 3. Canonical Capability Stages

Design/pacing uses five descriptive stages:

- S0 — Survival Core;
- S1 — Operational Outpost;
- S2 — Automated Industrial Hub;
- S3 — Fortified Research/Logistics Station;
- S4 — Strategic Nexus.

These are summaries, not numeric gates.

## 4. S0 — Survival Core

Starting Horizon includes or can rapidly restore:

- Command Core;
- minimal power;
- basic atmosphere/life support;
- basic storage;
- primitive fabrication/repair;
- one usable docking interface;
- basic communications/diagnostics.

Many systems are damaged, absent, or manually operated.

## 5. S0 Exit

The station can support repeatable Reach I expeditions when it has:

- stable minimum power;
- breathable crew-safe core area;
- usable docking/refit access;
- functional storage/logistics access;
- basic fabrication/repair;
- communication/navigation support.

No hidden station-stage flag substitutes for these conditions.

## 6. S1 — Operational Outpost

Typical capabilities:

- expanded habitation;
- dependable power distribution;
- basic farming/water recycling;
- refinery/fabricator chain;
- first specialized crew assignments;
- basic robot service/assembly;
- early external sensors/defenses;
- improved storage/logistics.

## 7. S2 — Automated Industrial Hub

Typical capabilities:

- multiple production chains;
- automated routine logistics;
- reliable agriculture/life support;
- Robot Assembly Bay;
- broader research facilities;
- ship service/refit capability;
- redundant power/storage;
- meaningful local shields/defenses;
- crew specialization and supervision.

## 8. S3 — Fortified Research/Logistics Station

Typical capabilities:

- advanced/fusion-scale power;
- advanced materials/robotics/defense production;
- layered station security;
- strong sensors/communications;
- distributed magazines/storage;
- redundant life support/thermal routes;
- advanced labs;
- support for larger ship classes and raid squads;
- resilient automation during player absence.

## 9. S4 — Strategic Nexus

Typical capabilities:

- Reach IV expedition support;
- advanced command/communication;
- high-end fabrication/research;
- robust layered defense;
- large multi-role robot fleet support;
- redundant critical infrastructure;
- faction/coalition logistics integration;
- ability to recover from severe station events without returning to manual survival for every routine process.

## 10. Stage Is Descriptive

A station can possess an unusual mix of S2/S3 capabilities.

The UI may summarize maturity but cannot use stage to magically enable a missing physical system.

## 11. Construction Progression

New station modules require their actual:

- Blueprint;
- Research;
- resources/components;
- structural connection;
- power/thermal/logistics prerequisites;
- build work.

## 12. Redundancy as Progression

A key late-game improvement is not simply larger output but reduced single points of failure.

Examples:

- dual power trunks;
- distributed batteries;
- redundant atmosphere processing;
- separated ammunition storage;
- multiple sensor nodes;
- emergency access routes.

## 13. Automation Progression

Early:
- player performs recurring tasks.

Middle:
- crew/robots execute routine tasks under configured policy.

Late:
- mature systems automatically resolve normal operation while player focuses on strategy, expansion, rare failures, missions, and raids.

Automation never creates resources or bypasses physical access.

## 14. Industry Progression

Industrial progression expands:

- material classes that can be processed;
- recipe complexity;
- throughput;
- parallel queues;
- quality/precision capability where specific designs require it;
- robot/ship component manufacturing.

It does not create generic Factory XP.

## 15. Research Progression

Research facilities improve available:

- domain capability;
- project complexity;
- analysis methods;
- scientist supervision capacity.

Research rules remain GDS-4 authority.

## 16. Crew Capacity

Station population can grow only with actual:

- habitation;
- atmosphere;
- water;
- food;
- medical/safety support.

Recruitment does not ignore station capacity.

## 17. Robot Fleet Capacity

Robot ownership/deployment is constrained by actual:

- assembly;
- storage;
- charging/service;
- maintenance;
- ammunition/resources;
- TCC for field deployment.

## 18. Defense Progression

Early defense:
- limited sensors/weapons and blind spots.

Middle:
- point defense, local shields, security robots, better magazines.

Late:
- layered coverage, redundancy, strong segmentation, hardened critical systems, automated response.

No Defense Score replaces the systems.

## 19. Layout Tradeoffs

Progression does not remove the consequences of station layout.

A technologically advanced station can remain vulnerable if:

- reactor/power routes are exposed;
- storage is concentrated;
- defenses have blind spots;
- logistics are fragile;
- security segmentation is poor.

## 20. Damage and Progression

Damage can temporarily reduce effective station capability without deleting the player's Research/Blueprint progression.

Repair/rebuild restores the physical capability.

## 21. Routine Raid Loss

GDS-11 can damage modules/steal resources/destroy robots, but cannot demote the station through a hidden Stage variable.

The station's real capabilities determine its post-raid state.

## 22. Resource Automation

By S2/S3 the station should be capable of reducing repeated manual acquisition pressure for common inputs through:

- recycling;
- production efficiency;
- trading;
- automated logistics;
- recurring supply operations where explicitly supported.

Rare strategic resources remain expedition/market/event drivers.

## 23. Spacecraft Support

Ship progression can require station service capabilities such as:

- hangar/dock size;
- refit tools;
- power;
- heavy component handling;
- advanced manufacturing.

Owning a Blueprint does not make Horizon able to fit a ship it cannot physically service.

## 24. Reach IV Readiness

S4 is not a mandatory named check for finale deployment.

The actual Reach IV/finale requirements are capability checks. A sufficiently specialized station can satisfy them even if some optional S4 features are absent.

## 25. Failure Recovery

Late station progression increases recovery resilience through real redundancy/automation rather than guaranteed immunity.

Severe attacks can still cause meaningful damage.

## 26. Postgame

Station expansion can continue after finale through:

- optimization;
- alternate module variants;
- redundancy;
- larger fleets;
- completion of side research;
- cosmetic/functional expansion.

No infinite Base Level is required.

## 27. Explicit Non-Goals

No Town Hall/Base Level gate, passive base XP, instant stage upgrade, hidden defense score, or automatic immunity from earlier hazards.

## 28. Tuneable Parameters

Module costs, build times, throughput, crew/robot capacity, defense output, and recovery rates are tuneable.

## 29. Dependencies

Depends on GDS-2 Station, GDS-3 Crew, GDS-4 Research/Resources, GDS-10 Robots, GDS-11 Defense, Economy, Research Progression, and Progression Model.

## 30. Open Questions

None in the station progression baseline.
