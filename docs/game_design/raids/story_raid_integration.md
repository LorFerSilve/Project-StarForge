# Main-Story Raid Integration

> **Status:** Draft  
> **Authority:** GDS-11 raid-phase execution for canonical GDS-7/GDS-8 story missions that can become fortified assaults

## 1. Purpose

GDS-7 owns story canon and GDS-8 owns main-story Mission IDs/objective structure.

This document fixes when GDS-11 raid mechanics apply to those missions so implementation does not decide ad hoc whether a hostile route is a raid.

## 2. Story Missions With GDS-11 Raid Authority

Baseline canonical applications:

1. **MS-A301 — Rook's Wake**: hostile assault branch against the fortified Blackwake base.
2. **MS-A302 — Sealed Authority**: GDS-11 applies only if the player chooses/causes a forced fortified assault path through Helios-controlled defenses; authorized/service access remains ordinary GDS-8 mission play.
3. **MS-F02 — Second Cascade**: final Crownless access/assault uses GDS-11 multi-phase raid mechanics where the player fights through fortified Nexus defenses.

Other missions may use isolated boarding/breaching/combat primitives without becoming full raids.

## 3. Full Raid Qualification

A story mission uses the full GDS-11 wrapper when the operation includes at least:

- a fortified target with persistent defenses/security;
- physical access creation or contested entry;
- interior/exterior systemic target interactions;
- attacker withdrawal/extraction risk.

A single locked door or one boarding encounter is not automatically a full Raid.

# MS-A301 — Rook's Wake

## 4. Canonical Target

Location:
- Kestrel System;
- **Rook's Wake**, the mobile/fortified Blackwake base.

Faction:
- Blackwake Corsairs.

GDS-8 still owns the mission's negotiated and hostile branches.

## 5. Negotiated Branch

The negotiated branch is **not** a Raid unless the player turns it hostile before the archive handoff resolves.

It uses ordinary mission/dialogue/transaction rules.

If violence escalates into an attempt to forcibly recover the archive from the fortified base, the mission transitions into the Hostile Raid branch using the same Mission ID.

## 6. Hostile Raid Primary Goal

Mandatory goal:

- recover and secure the designated Blackwake archive/source information;
- withdraw/extract.

Destroying Rook's Wake is not required.

## 7. Rook's Wake Raid Phases

### Phase A — Approach / Intelligence

Player approaches Rook's Wake with whatever Intel is legitimately known.

Potential valid access routes can include:

- compromised/smuggler docking route;
- direct defended dock;
- maintenance/EVA entry;
- created breach.

Exact available routes are authored target data.

### Phase B — External Access

If the selected route is contested, player must suppress/avoid/disable the local defenses necessary to reach the entry.

The raid does not require destruction of every Blackwake turret/ship.

### Phase C — Boarding / Bridgehead

Player physically enters Rook's Wake and can establish a Bridgehead.

Robot deployment remains constrained by the chosen entrance geometry.

### Phase D — Archive Recovery

Player must reach the designated archive carrier/section and obtain the persistent unique archive/source information.

Possible local methods can include:

- hacking access;
- physical data-carrier recovery;
- forced entry;
- defeating/forcing withdrawal of its custodian.

The archive identity cannot reroll.

### Phase E — Withdrawal

After archive security, player returns to a valid extraction route.

Blackwake escalation/reinforcement remains active.

### Phase F — Extraction

Hostile branch succeeds only after required archive state and extraction commit under GDS-8/GDS-11.

## 8. Rook Relationship Consequence

Captain Sable Rook's persistent relationship outcome remains GDS-7 authority.

GDS-11 supplies factual raid events such as:

- player identified;
- casualties;
- systems destroyed;
- archive stolen;
- negotiated branch betrayed.

Narrative/reputation systems convert those facts into consequences.

## 9. Rook's Wake Persistence

Committed raid damage/loot/access state persists under GDS-11 target-state rules unless a story transition explicitly moves/reconfigures the mobile base.

Any later relocation must preserve:

- destroyed/remaining unique assets;
- archive ownership;
- Captain Rook state;
- relevant faction memory.

# MS-A302 — Sealed Authority

## 10. Canonical Target

Location:
- Lyra System;
- **Authority Archive L-4**;
- within a Helios-claimed/restricted environment.

## 11. Non-Raid Paths

Authorized Helios access, documented permission, or a recovered service route can complete MS-A302 as ordinary Recovery/Data Retrieval.

These paths do not require GDS-11 full raid phases.

## 12. Forced Assault Transition

If the player deliberately forces access against active fortified Helios defense and the operation develops into:

- external defense engagement;
- forced boarding/breach;
- persistent interior resistance;
- contested extraction,

GDS-11 becomes the execution wrapper for the hostile portion while the Mission ID/objectives stay MS-A302.

## 13. Archive L-4 Hostile Goal

Mandatory story goal remains:

- obtain the required institutional emergency records;
- secure them;
- extract.

The player does not need to conquer the Helios installation.

## 14. Preserve-Archive Constraint

A hostile route cannot solve the mission by destroying the archive.

If the unique required archive is irrecoverably destroyed by player actions and no authored backup branch exists, the current attempt fails and story retry/reconciliation rules apply.

## 15. Helios Consequences

GDS-11 records attributable actions; GDS-7/GDS-12 applies reputation/security consequences.

A covert service-route recovery can therefore differ substantially from an openly destructive assault without changing the canonical archive result.

# MS-F02 — Second Cascade

## 16. Canonical Target

Location:
- Crownless System;
- **Crownless Lattice Nexus**.

This is the campaign's final Fracture-control/anomaly site.

## 17. Finale Raid Boundary

GDS-11 applies primarily to:

- Phase B — Gain Nexus Access;
- combat/sabotage/boarding portions of Phase C — Stabilize Control Path;
- withdrawal/survival portions of Phase E.

GDS-8 retains the overall five-phase mission structure and final-choice transaction.

## 18. Finale Raid Objective

The raid portion is not "destroy Crownless."

Its purpose is to create and hold a viable control path to the final decision interface while the Second Cascade is active.

## 19. Finale Phase A — Reach Crownless

GDS-6/GDS-8 owns Reach IV travel/local arrival.

GDS-11 begins once the player engages the fortified Nexus access problem.

## 20. Finale Phase B — Gain Nexus Access

Valid raid mechanics can include:

- external defense suppression;
- shield-zone manipulation;
- sensor/communication disruption;
- physical breach;
- Ancient/Continuance interface access;
- robot squad deployment.

The exact available paths must be authored in Crownless target content.

## 21. Finale Phase C — Stabilize Control Path

Player must secure the required system chain long enough to reach/activate the final decision interface.

Possible raid-relevant dependencies include:

- power/control nodes;
- anomaly containment interfaces;
- access/security;
- hostile machine systems;
- shield/defense support.

GDS-11 sabotage rules apply to these causal dependencies.

## 22. Coalition Support

MS-F01 coalition/support commitments can create additional physical capabilities such as:

- allied external ships;
- diversion;
- sensor/reinforcement support;
- supplied robots/equipment;
- route support.

Exact availability/cost is GDS-12/GDS-7 authority.

All support entering the raid must have a valid world/arrival path.

## 23. No Infinite Finale Reinforcements

Both hostile and allied finale forces are finite/authored for the Mission ID.

The finale cannot create endless waves merely to prolong play.

## 24. Final Choice Authority

GDS-11 cannot commit:

- Stabilize;
- Sever;
- Contain.

That choice belongs to the GDS-7/GDS-8 final-resolution transaction.

Raid systems only create/maintain access to the decision state.

## 25. Post-Choice Combat

After the choice commits, Phase E can require:

- survive system transition;
- withdraw from collapsing/hostile areas;
- defend a node;
- reach extraction/safe-resolution point.

The chosen ending state cannot be changed by a later combat hit unless the authored ending explicitly includes a failure window before final success commit.

## 26. Finale Failure

Routine tactical defeat before final success remains retryable under GDS-7/GDS-8.

Robot losses, ammo/ship damage, and other ordinary persistence follow the current save/retry policy finalized by GDS-12.

## 27. Story Raid Loot

Story raids can contain optional physical loot, but no optional raid loot may duplicate or substitute mandatory unique story evidence unless the story specification explicitly says so.

## 28. Story Raid Target Reset

Story retry must not use a generic pristine reset that duplicates committed unique objects or creates contradictory character states.

Any authored retry normalization must define exactly what is restored for playability versus what remains canonically committed.

## 29. Explicit Non-Goals

Story raid integration does not:

- turn every hostile story encounter into a full raid;
- make destruction of the entire station mandatory;
- allow GDS-11 to redefine story outcomes;
- create alternate fourth finale ending;
- grant infinite finale forces;
- permit archive duplication on retry.

## 30. Dependencies

This specification depends on GDS-7 Canonical Region Map/Main Story, GDS-8 Story Mission Execution, all GDS-11 raid-phase specifications, GDS-9 Combat, GDS-10 Robots, and future GDS-12 Progression/Persistence.

## 31. Open Questions

None at the GDS-11 story-raid integration level.

Exact room layouts, enemy counts, weapon values, and finale force composition remain tuneable/content data within these fixed contracts.
