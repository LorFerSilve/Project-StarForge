# Environmental Storytelling

> **Status:** Draft  
> **Authority:** Rules for conveying historical/narrative information through physical spaces, persistent damage, props, logs, system state, and location composition

## 1. Purpose

The world should communicate what happened through spaces the player explores, not only through dialogue.

## 2. Principle

Environmental storytelling must be consistent with actual gameplay systems.

A location suggesting decompression should show plausible:

- breach;
- sealed door;
- emergency equipment;
- pressure-state history.

## 3. Storytelling Channels

Baseline channels:

- structural damage;
- abandoned equipment;
- resource depletion;
- improvised repairs;
- emergency barriers;
- bodies/remains where appropriate;
- personal objects;
- signage;
- terminal logs;
- machinery state;
- faction modifications.

## 4. Systemic Evidence

Real system states can carry narrative meaning.

Examples:

- one reactor manually isolated during disaster;
- emergency battery intentionally preserved;
- airlock welded shut;
- cargo deliberately jettisoned;
- Continuance node maintaining one compartment.

## 5. Logs

Logs can be:

- operational;
- personal;
- scientific;
- military;
- automated.

Logs should usually be short and context-linked.

## 6. No Mandatory Log Hunting for Core Rules

Critical gameplay rules are not hidden exclusively in optional lore logs.

Main-story required evidence is placed on mandatory/clearly identified paths.

## 7. Faction Visual History

A pre-Fracture site reused by a faction should visibly show layers:

Original structure  
+ post-Fracture repair  
+ current faction modifications.

## 8. Procedural Locations

Procedural storytelling uses authored templates with internally coherent story states.

Random prop placement cannot create contradictions such as:
- evacuation signs leading into impossible geometry;
- civilian colony containing military-only security without context.

## 9. Unique Locations

Major story locations use authored narrative composition.

## 10. Ancient Structures

Ancient locations should visually differ from human infrastructure.

Their presentation must avoid directly explaining their origin before narrative evidence does.

## 11. Continuance Sites

Continuance-controlled infrastructure should communicate machine priorities through:
- maintained critical routes;
- sealed forbidden zones;
- preserved machinery;
- absent human comfort maintenance.

## 12. Before/After State

Important locations can visibly change after player actions.

Examples:
- lights restored;
- civilians return;
- defenses repaired;
- route relay active;
- faction banners change.

## 13. Readability

Interactable narrative evidence should be discoverable through:
- composition;
- scanner;
- lighting;
- signage;
- audio.

It should not rely on pixel hunting.

## 14. Environmental Misdirection

A scene may support a mistaken initial interpretation, but later evidence must make the true interpretation coherent.

## 15. Persistence

Unique recovered logs/evidence record persistent acquisition.

The physical world state updates according to the relevant location outcome.

## 16. Explicit Non-Goals

Environmental storytelling does not require:
- every object having lore;
- optional logs containing indispensable control instructions;
- random clutter without context;
- contradictory fake environmental evidence solely to surprise the player.

## 17. Dependencies

This specification depends on World locations, Lore, Missions, Presentation, station/ship systems, and Research Evidence.

## 18. Open Questions

None in the environmental-storytelling baseline.
