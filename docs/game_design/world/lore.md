# Historical Lore

> **Status:** Design Complete  
> **Authority:** Canonical historical timeline, pre-Fracture civilization, The Fracture chronology, post-Fracture political emergence, and historical truths discoverable by the player

## 1. Purpose

Lore gives the world a consistent history that explains why stations, factions, derelicts, technology loss, survivors, and route progression exist.

## 2. Calendar

The main historical notation is:

- BF — Before Fracture;
- AF — After Fracture.

The game begins in 67 AF.

## 3. Pre-Fracture Expansion

### 190–120 BF

Humanity establishes sustained interstellar settlements through increasingly efficient propulsion and route computation.

Colonies initially remain expensive and politically dependent.

## 4. Network Era

### 120–35 BF

Interstellar civilization becomes highly connected.

Major developments:

- standardized route maps;
- autonomous cargo;
- distributed industrial stations;
- large orbital habitats;
- advanced machine control;
- fusion infrastructure;
- regional research networks.

## 5. Late Network Era

### 35–5 BF

Civilization increasingly relies on a shared navigation/control architecture informally called the **Transit Lattice**.

The Transit Lattice is not one physical machine.

It is an interdependent ecosystem of:

- navigation observatories;
- route relays;
- timing/reference infrastructure;
- autonomous traffic control;
- anomaly prediction systems;
- high-energy experimental route technology.

## 6. Pre-Fracture Research

During the final decades, several research programs investigate anomalous spatial phenomena and non-human structures.

Records show collaboration and rivalry between predecessors of:

- Asterion;
- defense authorities;
- industrial operators;
- autonomous infrastructure agencies.

## 7. The Fracture — 0 AF

Within hours, major Transit Lattice routes become unreliable or unusable across multiple regions.

Recorded events include:

- contradictory navigation solutions;
- communication blackouts;
- automated route closures;
- ship disappearances/losses;
- high-energy station failures;
- unexplained anomaly signatures.

## 8. Emergency Response

Defense fleets impose route lockdowns.

Research institutions attempt emergency shutdowns.

Industrial networks isolate local infrastructure.

Autonomous systems receive overlapping preservation/containment directives.

## 9. Continuance Origin

The systems later called **The Continuance** originate from pre-Fracture autonomous infrastructure networks tasked with:

- maintaining life-supporting infrastructure;
- preserving strategic systems;
- containing hazardous technology;
- restoring route services when safe.

The Fracture caused these directives to conflict across disconnected nodes.

## 10. Collapse Period

### 0–12 AF

Without stable routes:

- colonies lose supply chains;
- stations fail;
- governments fragment;
- piracy expands;
- local production becomes essential.

Millions survive, but interstellar society contracts sharply.

## 11. Consolidation Period

### 12–35 AF

Major post-Fracture factions emerge:

- Helios Directorate from defense/emergency governments;
- Meridian Compact from industrial/logistics networks;
- Asterion Institute from surviving research institutions;
- Freehold Coalition from autonomous settlements;
- Blackwake Corsairs from fragmented fleets, smugglers, raiders, and defectors.

## 12. Recovery Period

### 35–67 AF

Limited routes are remapped.

Regional trade returns.

New generations grow up without unified interstellar civilization.

Recovered technology becomes strategically important.

## 13. The Unknown Structures

Evidence of non-standard structures predates confirmed human settlement in several systems.

Their relationship to The Fracture is unknown at game start.

They are called **Ancient Structures** as a practical label, not a confirmed civilization name.

## 14. Historical Disagreement

Different factions teach different explanations.

### Helios

The Fracture proves uncontrolled experimental technology and weak governance are existential risks.

### Meridian

The collapse was worsened by overcentralized route infrastructure and poor redundancy.

### Asterion

The cause remains scientifically unresolved and must be investigated.

### Freehold

The disaster became civilization-ending because communities were too dependent on distant centralized systems.

### Blackwake

Official histories conceal responsibility and protect the institutions that caused the disaster.

### Continuance

Node records are incomplete, contradictory, or access-restricted.

## 15. Canonical Hidden Truth Structure

The narrative will eventually establish that The Fracture resulted from **multiple interacting causes**, not one simplistic villain button.

The fixed high-level truth is:

1. Late pre-Fracture researchers attempted controlled use of anomalous route physics.
2. The Transit Lattice became more tightly integrated with those predictive systems than the public understood.
3. A severe anomaly event produced cascading invalid route/control states.
4. Human emergency interventions and autonomous containment directives amplified parts of the cascade.
5. Some Continuance nodes prevented worse outcomes while others caused additional losses.
6. Ancient technology/anomalous structures influenced the event, but were not a conventional invading army.
7. Several institutions later suppressed records to prevent repetition and avoid political accountability.

## 16. No Single Secret Villain

No one faction or individual is solely responsible for The Fracture.

Specific people/institutions can still bear responsibility for dangerous decisions or coverups.

## 17. Continuing Threat

The underlying anomaly mechanism remains capable of reappearing under specific conditions.

The main story therefore concerns not only historical truth but preventing or controlling a second large-scale cascade.

## 18. Recoverable History

The player can learn history through:

- station archives;
- data carriers;
- survivor testimony;
- faction records;
- derelicts;
- Continuance nodes;
- research evidence;
- Ancient Structures.

## 19. Lore Reliability

Sources can be:

- accurate;
- incomplete;
- biased;
- deliberately falsified.

The authoritative canon remains this document even when in-world characters are wrong.

## 20. Environmental History

Locations should visually support their history.

Examples:

- emergency-sealed corridors;
- abandoned evacuation docks;
- improvised post-Fracture repairs;
- faction reuse of pre-Fracture structures;
- route relays frozen in lockdown state.

## 21. Explicit Non-Goals

Lore does not use:

- one all-powerful evil mastermind;
- an unexplained total human extinction;
- aliens invading purely to provide enemies;
- retcons that contradict the core Fracture chronology without formal design change.

## 22. Dependencies

This specification depends on Setting, Factions, Narrative, Ancient/Anomaly content, Research, and World locations.

## 23. Open Questions

None in the historical baseline.

Specific named pre-Fracture individuals and facilities are authored in Narrative/location content.
