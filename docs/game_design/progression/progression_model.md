# Progression Model

> **Status:** Design Complete  
> **Authority:** Cross-domain progression structure, campaign capability phases, phase gates, anti-grind rules, horizontal/vertical advancement, and progression invariants

## 1. Purpose

Progression describes how the player grows from a damaged local foothold into a capable interstellar command operation without introducing a generic Player Level or universal Power Score.

## 2. Progression Is Capability-Based

Progression comes from actual state in several domains:

- player equipment/tools;
- Horizon Station infrastructure;
- crew skills/coverage;
- spacecraft and Reach;
- robots/TCC;
- Research;
- Blueprints;
- industry/resources;
- faction relations;
- discovered routes/world knowledge.

## 3. No Universal Level

There is no single Player Level, Account Level, Gear Score, Combat Rating, or Base Level that determines what the world becomes.

## 4. Capability Gates

A gate is satisfied by explicit conditions such as:

- required Research complete;
- Blueprint owned;
- physical module installed/operational;
- Reach capability present;
- required route verified;
- mission/story state complete;
- faction access obtained;
- crew qualification available.

## 5. No Hidden Power Gate

A mission cannot require "Power 5,000" unless such a metric is separately defined for a narrow explicit purpose.

Baseline campaign gates use real capabilities.

## 6. Canonical Progression Phases

The base campaign uses six cross-domain phases:

- **P0 — Recovery / Recommissioning**;
- **P1 — Local Operator / Reach I**;
- **P2 — Frontier Network / Reach II**;
- **P3 — Strategic Power / Reach III**;
- **P4 — Verge Expedition / Reach IV**;
- **P5 — Second Cascade Resolution / Postgame**.

These are design/pacing states, not XP levels.

## 7. Phase P0 — Recovery / Recommissioning

Player state:

- Horizon Station damaged and minimally functional;
- starter equipment/tools;
- low-capability Shuttle/Scout access;
- no mature robot force;
- small/no recruited specialist roster;
- Basic Fabrication available;
- focus on restoring minimum power, atmosphere, storage, fabrication, docking, communications.

Exit principle:

Horizon can support repeatable local expeditions and the player can safely operate within Reach I.

## 8. Phase P1 — Local Operator / Reach I

World focus:

- Recovery Sector;
- Aster's Rest;
- Caldera;
- Morrow.

Capabilities mature around:

- local mining/salvage;
- first survivor recruitment;
- early station automation;
- basic weapons/suit improvement;
- early robot manufacturing/support;
- verified local route network.

Exit principle:

The player has established outside contact and obtained the knowledge/capability required to enter Reach II progression.

## 9. Phase P2 — Frontier Network / Reach II

World focus:

- Inner Frontier;
- Vesper;
- Bastion;
- Kara;
- Orison;
- associated faction hubs.

Capabilities mature around:

- faction trade/services;
- more specialized research;
- stronger station industry;
- automated farming/logistics/repair;
- broader robot squad;
- advanced shields/materials;
- improved ship configurations.

Exit principle:

The player acquires the research, evidence, route access, ship capability, and institutional contacts needed for Reach III operations.

## 10. Phase P3 — Strategic Power / Reach III

World focus:

- Contested Reach;
- Outer Expanse access;
- Kestrel;
- Lyra;
- Nacre;
- Nadir;
- Pale Crown.

Capabilities mature around:

- fusion/advanced energy;
- advanced robotics;
- fortified Horizon defenses;
- serious raids;
- heavy/high-specialization robot options;
- higher-end spacecraft/refit;
- quantum/anomaly research foundation.

Exit principle:

The Fracture mechanism is sufficiently understood and Reach IV expedition requirements become satisfiable.

## 11. Phase P4 — Verge Expedition / Reach IV

World focus:

- Eidolon;
- Threshold;
- Null Choir;
- Crownless route preparation.

Capabilities mature around:

- Reach IV propulsion/navigation;
- Ancient/Anomaly system understanding;
- robust expedition endurance;
- advanced station/ship/robot engineering;
- coalition preparation.

Exit principle:

The player can deploy MS-F02 with all mandatory physical and strategic requirements satisfied.

## 12. Phase P5 — Resolution / Postgame

Starts after the Second Cascade finale commits.

The player retains:

- Horizon Station;
- world exploration;
- unfinished side missions;
- trading;
- production;
- raids/dynamic events as compatible with ending state;
- further station/ship/robot optimization.

There is no mandatory reset/prestige loop.

## 13. Phase Advancement

Phase advancement is triggered by explicit campaign capability/story commits.

It is never triggered by filling a generic XP bar.

## 14. Phase Does Not Grant Free Gear

Entering a new phase does not automatically spawn:

- equipment;
- ships;
- robots;
- station modules;
- resources.

It unlocks/reflects access to possibilities already justified by story/research/routes/factions.

## 15. Horizontal Progression

Horizontal progression gives alternative capability/configurations, such as:

- ballistic vs energy weapons;
- combat vs mining ship fitting;
- different robot squad composition;
- redundant vs compact station layout;
- faction-specific module variants.

It should remain useful even after later phases.

## 16. Vertical Progression

Vertical progression increases actual capability through better technology/infrastructure, such as:

- stronger power systems;
- more capable sensors;
- higher Reach;
- more efficient processing;
- higher TCC;
- advanced armor/shields.

Vertical advancement must still preserve earlier content usefulness where plausible.

## 17. No Automatic Obsolescence

A small Scout, Sidearm, Recon Drone, or simple station module does not become unusable merely because a later phase exists.

Later technology can outperform it in selected roles, but lower-cost/low-mass/low-power designs retain legitimate niches.

## 18. Inter-domain Dependency

Major progression intentionally requires several systems to reinforce each other.

Example:

Reach IV may require:

Research Evidence → Research → Blueprint/design access → advanced components → station manufacturing → valid ship fitting → verified route.

No one system should bypass the entire chain.

## 19. Common-Resource Automation

As progression advances, repetitive acquisition of common inputs should increasingly be handled by:

- station recycling/industry;
- trade;
- automated resource missions where explicitly supported;
- logistics/crew/robots.

The player should focus increasingly on strategic materials, discoveries, decisions, and high-value operations.

## 20. No Grind Gate

Mandatory progression must not require repeating the exact same low-value mission solely to fill an arbitrary numeric bar.

Resource needs, research work, crew experience, and economic preparation may require activity, but each has a systemic purpose.

## 21. No Difficulty Gate

All progression phases/endings are available on every Difficulty profile.

## 22. Reputation and Progression

Faction reputation can unlock access/alternatives but the core campaign retains convergent alternate paths if one faction becomes hostile.

No single human faction relationship can irreversibly softlock the main story through ordinary play.

## 23. Failure and Progression

Failure can damage current capability materially:

- robots lost;
- resources spent/stolen;
- station/ship damaged;
- reputation worsened.

It does not erase committed Research/Blueprint/story knowledge.

## 24. Postgame

Postgame is continuation of the same persistent world after the selected Stabilize/Sever/Contain result.

No New Game+ is required for baseline.

## 25. No Endless Prestige

The baseline has no infinite prestige levels, paragon points, seasonal reset, or repeatable stat ladder.

## 26. Progression Readability

GDS-13 must make blockers explicit:

- what capability is missing;
- where it can come from if known;
- which prerequisite chain is incomplete.

The implementation must not reduce that to unexplained "level too low."

## 27. Persistence

Phase state and every underlying gate condition persist.

Save/load cannot relock legitimate completed progression.

## 28. Explicit Non-Goals

Progression does not use:

- Player Level;
- Gear Score;
- generic Base Level;
- infinite prestige;
- forced seasonal reset;
- difficulty-exclusive content;
- mandatory repetitive XP grind.

## 29. Tuneable Parameters

Resource costs, Research Work, XP thresholds inside crew skills, economic prices, and exact balancing are tuneable.

The six-phase structure and capability-based gate philosophy are fixed.

## 30. Dependencies

This specification integrates GDS-2 through GDS-12 and is detailed by the progression-domain specifications.

## 31. Open Questions

None in the cross-domain progression model.
