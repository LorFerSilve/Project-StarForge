# Game Overview

> **Status:** Draft  
> **Authority:** High-level game identity and core gameplay structure

## 1. Working Title

**Project StarForge**

The title is currently a working project name.

## 2. Genre

Project StarForge is a 3D science-fiction game combining:

- action;
- exploration;
- mission-based resource gathering;
- crafting;
- station construction;
- station engineering;
- automation;
- strategic defense;
- robot squad management;
- hostile station raids;
- long-term progression.

## 3. Core Fantasy

The player begins with a small, damaged orbital foothold and limited technology.

Through expeditions, resource acquisition, survivor recruitment, research, manufacturing, ship development, automation, and combat, that foothold grows into a large self-sustaining interstellar station and strategic stronghold.

The long-term fantasy is the transformation from:

> **hands-on survivor and field operator**

into:

> **commander of a functioning interstellar operation**

without removing the player's ability to personally explore, fight, repair, build, and participate in raids.

## 4. Player-Owned Space Station

The player's home station is the central persistent location and progression anchor.

It is not represented only through menus.

The player physically traverses the station and interacts with its functional spaces.

The station can contain systems and modules for:

- command;
- power generation;
- power distribution;
- atmosphere and oxygen;
- thermal management;
- water;
- farming and food;
- storage;
- logistics;
- refining;
- fabrication;
- research;
- medicine;
- crew habitation;
- robotics;
- ship docking;
- ship maintenance;
- sensing;
- communications;
- shielding;
- defensive weapons;
- security.

Station layout has functional consequences.

Infrastructure is physically and logically connected rather than modeled as unrelated buildings with independent bonuses.

## 5. Expeditions

The player leaves the station to perform bounded 3D missions at locations such as:

- planets;
- moons;
- asteroid fields;
- abandoned spacecraft;
- derelict stations;
- colonies;
- mining facilities;
- research installations;
- hostile bases;
- unknown structures.

Expeditions can provide:

- raw resources;
- processed components;
- blueprints;
- research data;
- equipment;
- technology;
- intelligence;
- survivors;
- faction consequences;
- access to further locations.

## 6. Recruitable Survivors and Crew

Survivors can be encountered during expeditions and other world interactions.

Some survivors can be recruited to the player's station.

Recruited crew are persistent station inhabitants rather than abstract stat cards.

Crew members can specialize in operational roles such as:

- engineering;
- farming;
- research;
- medicine;
- robotics;
- mechanics;
- logistics;
- security;
- weapons;
- navigation;
- intelligence.

Crew progression primarily enables the station to move from manual operation toward reliable automation.

## 7. Automation

Automation is a central progression theme.

Early in the game, the player personally performs many operational tasks.

As the station develops, crew, robotics, specialized machinery, and later advanced control systems can take over routine work.

Automation must not make the station irrelevant. It changes the player's role from manually handling every process to configuring, supervising, expanding, and responding to exceptional problems.

## 8. Spacecraft

The player uses spacecraft to travel between mission locations and strategic regions.

Ships are modular and contain meaningful systems such as:

- propulsion;
- power;
- shields;
- armor;
- weapons;
- cargo;
- sensors;
- life support;
- utility equipment.

Different ship configurations support different roles.

Propulsion development is also a progression gate that expands accessible space.

## 9. Robots

The player can manufacture robotic units for combat and operational use.

During raids and selected missions, robots can be deployed as a limited tactical squad.

The player personally participates rather than controlling the game exclusively as an RTS.

Robot commands remain tactical and readable.

## 10. Hostile Station Raids

Raids combine strategic preparation and direct action.

A raid can include:

1. intelligence gathering;
2. target analysis;
3. ship and squad preparation;
4. attack on external defenses;
5. boarding or breaching;
6. interior combat;
7. infrastructure sabotage;
8. resource or objective acquisition;
9. extraction.

Station systems create strategic targets.

For example, disabling a power relay can be more valuable than individually destroying every defense connected to it.

## 11. Station Defense

The player's station is also a defensible space.

Defensive design includes:

- physical layout;
- protected critical systems;
- redundant infrastructure;
- shields;
- external weapons;
- point defense;
- security systems;
- defensive robots;
- compartmentalization.

The game should reward intelligent system design rather than only accumulation of higher defensive statistics.

## 12. Core Gameplay Loop

```text
Evaluate station needs and long-term goals
            ↓
Select expedition, objective, or raid
            ↓
Prepare player equipment, ship, and applicable robots
            ↓
Travel to mission location
            ↓
Explore / fight / mine / salvage / rescue / investigate
            ↓
Acquire resources / technology / survivors / intelligence
            ↓
Return to station
            ↓
Process / craft / research / recruit / repair
            ↓
Build and automate station
            ↓
Upgrade player / ships / robots / infrastructure
            ↓
Unlock more difficult opportunities
            ↓
Repeat
```

## 13. Progression Domains

Project StarForge contains interconnected progression in:

- player equipment;
- spacecraft;
- station infrastructure;
- crew;
- automation;
- robots;
- research;
- resources and industry;
- factions;
- galaxy access.

These domains should reinforce each other rather than behave as isolated leveling tracks.

## 14. Scope Philosophy

The game should feel large without depending on technically disproportionate simulation.

The project favors:

- bounded mission zones over seamless full-size planets;
- meaningful robot squads over thousands of simultaneous troops;
- selective destruction over unrestricted voxel destruction;
- connected station simulation over universal physical simulation;
- targeted procedural generation over procedural generation for its own sake.

## 15. Current High-Level Non-Goals

Unless explicitly changed by later accepted design decisions, the project is not currently designed around:

- MMO-scale persistent multiplayer;
- billions of seamless procedural planets;
- unrestricted voxel destruction;
- photorealism as a primary requirement;
- full real-world orbital simulation;
- thousands of simultaneous tactical combat units;
- a general-purpose reusable game engine as the project's primary product.

## 16. Open Design Work

This overview establishes game identity but intentionally does not define the detailed rules of every subsystem.

Those rules will be specified in their authoritative thematic documents before implementation.
