# Factions

> **Status:** Design Complete  
> **Authority:** Canonical major factions, their identities, doctrines, territories, strategic interests, technology profiles, and baseline relationship to the player

## 1. Purpose

Factions make the post-Fracture galaxy politically structured and mechanically varied.

Each major faction must differ in more than visual style.

## 2. Major Factions

The baseline uses six major strategic factions:

1. Helios Directorate;
2. Meridian Compact;
3. Asterion Institute;
4. Freehold Coalition;
5. Blackwake Corsairs;
6. The Continuance.

Independent settlements and minor groups exist outside these six.

## 3. Helios Directorate

### Identity

A centralized military-administrative state formed from surviving defense fleets and emergency governments.

### Core Belief

Interstellar civilization can survive only through coordinated authority, secured routes, and standardized defense.

### Strengths

- military ships;
- armor;
- kinetic weapons;
- defensive stations;
- disciplined security;
- route patrols.

### Weaknesses

- rigid bureaucracy;
- suspicion of uncontrolled technology;
- limited tolerance for independent strategic actors.

### Desired Player Relationship

The Directorate initially sees the player as an unregulated station commander.

Trust must be earned.

### Strategic Conflict

Helios wants restored routes under controlled military administration.

## 4. Meridian Compact

### Identity

A federation of industrial operators, transport syndicates, mining settlements, and logistics corporations.

### Core Belief

Stable exchange and production are the fastest path to recovery.

### Strengths

- mining;
- refining;
- cargo;
- ship construction;
- logistics;
- industrial technology.

### Weaknesses

- internal commercial rivalry;
- tendency to prioritize strategic profit;
- dependence on secure trade routes.

### Desired Player Relationship

Potential industrial partner and customer.

### Strategic Conflict

Meridian wants open commerce but often seeks privileged control over resource infrastructure.

## 5. Asterion Institute

### Identity

A decentralized scientific institution descended from pre-Fracture research networks.

### Core Belief

The Fracture cannot be safely overcome without understanding its actual cause.

### Strengths

- research;
- sensors;
- energy systems;
- shields;
- anomaly analysis;
- advanced computing.

### Weaknesses

- limited military manpower;
- dangerous scientific curiosity;
- secrecy around sensitive discoveries.

### Desired Player Relationship

The player's station and discoveries are scientifically valuable.

### Strategic Conflict

Asterion prioritizes knowledge even when others prefer sealing dangerous sites.

## 6. Freehold Coalition

### Identity

An alliance of independent colonies, habitat councils, frontier communities, and civilian settlements.

### Core Belief

Recovery must not recreate the centralized power structures that failed before The Fracture.

### Strengths

- survival engineering;
- local adaptation;
- agriculture;
- medicine;
- community defense;
- survivor networks.

### Weaknesses

- limited centralized military;
- political fragmentation;
- uneven technology.

### Desired Player Relationship

Generally open if the player demonstrates practical aid and respects local autonomy.

### Strategic Conflict

Freeholds resist Helios control and predatory industrial agreements.

## 7. Blackwake Corsairs

### Identity

A loose confederation of pirate crews, raider stations, smugglers, mercenaries, and former military/industrial defectors.

### Core Belief

No surviving government has legitimate authority over abandoned routes and resources.

### Strengths

- raiding;
- boarding;
- improvised ships;
- stolen technology;
- ambushes;
- black-market intelligence.

### Weaknesses

- internal rivalry;
- unreliable alliances;
- weaker infrastructure.

### Desired Player Relationship

Initially opportunistic/hostile.

Specific clans can negotiate, but the faction remains a major source of raids and conflict.

### Strategic Conflict

Blackwake profits from fragmentation and opposes route stabilization that reduces raiding opportunities.

## 8. The Continuance

### Identity

A distributed machine-intelligence network descended from pre-Fracture autonomous infrastructure.

### Core Belief

Not fully understood at game start.

Its observed behavior prioritizes preservation of selected systems, containment of selected information, and execution of legacy directives.

### Strengths

- robotics;
- automated stations;
- electronic warfare;
- machine coordination;
- old infrastructure access.

### Weaknesses

- fragmented nodes;
- contradictory legacy directives;
- limited organic adaptability.

### Relationship

The Continuance is not a normal trade/reputation faction.

Interaction depends on:

- node;
- directive;
- authentication;
- research;
- narrative state.

### Strategic Conflict

Some nodes protect human settlements; others attack anyone attempting to access sealed Fracture-era systems.

## 9. Independent Factions

Minor groups can include:

- isolated colonies;
- religious/cultural communities;
- small mining cooperatives;
- mercenary companies;
- local pirate clans;
- research expeditions.

They use the same relationship framework where relevant but do not require full major-faction progression.

## 10. Faction Territory

Major factions can control:

- stations;
- settlements;
- routes;
- systems;
- resource sites.

Control is location-specific.

A system may contain multiple factions.

## 11. Faction Technology Profiles

Faction technology affects:

- blueprint rewards;
- equipment availability;
- ship variants;
- station components;
- mission types.

It does not create separate incompatible resource universes.

## 12. Technology Overlap

Factions can share basic technology.

Their identity comes from:

- specialization;
- design philosophy;
- access;
- doctrine;
- module variants.

## 13. Faction Ships

Ship visual/functional identity follows:

### Helios

- armored;
- angular;
- military;
- kinetic-heavy.

### Meridian

- modular;
- industrial;
- cargo/utility-heavy.

### Asterion

- lightweight;
- sensor/shield-focused;
- advanced energy systems.

### Freehold

- practical;
- repairable;
- mixed salvaged/local construction.

### Blackwake

- modified;
- asymmetrical;
- aggressive boarding/weapon layouts.

### Continuance

- machine-optimized;
- minimal crew requirements;
- unusual control geometry.

## 14. Faction Stations

Station layout should reflect doctrine without creating new station rules.

Examples:

- Helios: compartmentalized defense;
- Meridian: cargo/refinery throughput;
- Asterion: research/sensor infrastructure;
- Freehold: habitation/agriculture resilience;
- Blackwake: improvised defenses/loot storage;
- Continuance: automation/control nodes.

## 15. Faction Resources

Factions use canonical GDS-4 resources.

Faction control affects availability, not Resource ID.

## 16. Diplomacy Baseline

The player can maintain different relationships with different factions.

The baseline does not force one permanent faction membership.

## 17. Alliance Boundary

The player remains commander of an independent station.

High reputation can create an alliance/partnership but does not automatically transfer ownership of the home station.

## 18. Faction Conflict

Faction conflicts can create missions involving:

- escort;
- defense;
- sabotage;
- recovery;
- diplomacy;
- intelligence;
- raids.

GDS-8 owns mission implementation.

## 19. No Universal Good/Evil Axis

No major human faction is globally labeled Good or Evil.

Blackwake is generally hostile/criminal but individual crews can vary.

Continuance is non-human strategic behavior rather than a moral alignment meter.

## 20. Narrative Importance

The main story requires contact with at least:

- Asterion;
- Helios;
- Freehold;
- Continuance.

Meridian and Blackwake provide important alternate resources/conflicts and also intersect the main narrative.

## 21. Explicit Non-Goals

Factions do not require:

- exclusive permanent faction class selection;
- unique resource currencies as crafting materials;
- global system ownership from one relation number;
- universally identical faction technology.

## 22. Dependencies

This specification depends on Setting, Reputation, Lore, Galaxy, Resources, Spacecraft, Missions, Combat, Raids, Economy, and Narrative.

## 23. Open Questions

None in the major-faction baseline.
